Why Pausa

Most break reminders fire at the wrong moment and are easy to ignore. Pausa fixes both.

It knows when you’re busy

In a call, playing media, or on a muted video tab? The countdown pauses automatically and resumes when you’re free — meeting time never counts toward your next break.

Impossible to miss

A native fullscreen panel covers fullscreen Spaces and every monitor, then returns focus to exactly where you were. No fragile “always on top” windows.

Respects your hours

Breaks only fire on the days and times you choose, and real away-from-keyboard time counts as a natural break so you aren’t nagged after a walk.

Private by default

No account, no telemetry, and no automatic network calls — the only request is the update check you trigger. Busy detection runs locally on your Mac. Read more.

Privacy

  • No account and no sign-in.
  • No analytics or telemetry.
  • No automatic network requests — configuration and logs stay on disk. The only network call is the optional "Check for updates" action, run once and only when you click it.
  • Local busy detection — it checks whether the microphone is in use (not the audio itself), system media state, and only the frontmost browser’s active tab URL. None of it leaves your machine.

FAQ

Does Pausa need Accessibility or Screen Recording permission?

No. It uses public system APIs plus a short, read-only AppleScript lookup for the frontmost browser’s active tab URL. It never records your screen or keystrokes.

Does it collect any data?

No. There is no telemetry. Pausa makes no automatic network requests; the only one is the update check you trigger.

Will it interrupt me during a call?

No. Microphone use auto-pauses the schedule, and the countdown resumes when the call ends.

Why does macOS warn me on first launch?

Pausa isn’t notarized yet. Right-click the app → Open, or run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/pausa.app. Notarization is on the roadmap.

Does it work with fullscreen apps?

Yes. Break overlays are native panels rendered above fullscreen Spaces, and can cover every monitor.
